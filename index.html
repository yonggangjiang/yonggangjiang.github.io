Research Interests

I work broadly on algorithms and optimization. My goal is to design algorithms that are theoretically efficient and fast in practice. I am particularly interested in:

  • Graph algorithms
  • Parallel computing
  • Algorithm engineering
  • AI for mathematics and optimization

I am currently developing LLM harnesses for mathematical research. I lead the development of TCS-prover and contribute to ProofCouncil.

My personal opinions on papers with AI-generated results

I distinguish two kinds of contribution in theoretical papers:

  1. Introducing a new concept, problem, model, or conjecture, and explaining why it is useful or important.
  2. Finding a new solution to an established open problem on which progress has been difficult.

Traditionally, progress on open problems was usually scarce, so 2 was often a paper’s main contribution.

Recently, AI has become powerful at 2 (see OpenAI’s report, as one of the examples). However, many recent theoretical papers still follow the traditional framing: they present 2 as their main contribution, even when the solution is produced by AI and the paper contributes nothing of 1. I mark such papers with AI . Note that I do not use this marker for papers whose main contribution is a new human-developed concept, problem, model, or conjecture, even if their proofs are generated by AI and they are extracted from AI proofs.

Personally, I see papers with this marker primarily as communicating what is now known about existing open problems. This is important: sharing these results helps others avoid duplicating effort and allows the community to move forward. I also count polishing the writing and making the results understandable to humans as a contribution. However, I do not claim intellectual credit for these papers; that credit goes to AI. I would rather see more intellectual effort spent on 1, where we can make real contributions.
